# Real-time agent steering: prior art and the case for a closed-loop intervention benchmark

October 2026. This review covers what the jit-eval codebase formalizes, what its experiments have shown, the research literature on runtime supervision of LLM agents, an assessment of what is novel, and a proposal for a benchmark that treats the intervention policy, not the agent or the decision model, as the object of evaluation.

## 0. Scope and method

- **Codebase and results.** The framework, its experimental axes and its results were read from `README.md`, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, `agents/*`, `experiment_config.py`, `scripts/*`, `experiments/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` and `results/*`.
- **Literature search.** Research agents each searched one slice of the literature: direct prior art with citation chasing; tau-bench and policy adherence; runtime enforcement, guardrails and human approval; AI control and monitoring; verifiers, critics and intervention harm; small and fast supervisors, routing and calibration; guidance delivery and context rewriting; evaluation methodology; decision models and the Decision Index. Searches ran through the arXiv API (roughly 750 queries and lookups through a shared, rate-limited client), arXiv full-text pages for close reading, Semantic Scholar for citations of the closest papers, and the Hugging Face papers index.
- **Verification.** Every arXiv ID in this document was looked up through the arXiv API and exists, with the cited title. The closest papers were also read directly: 2608.12654, 2602.03338, 2603.19328, 2608.19303, 2605.22166, 2608.21027, 2606.21399, 2604.27233, 2609.05736, 2608.08239, 2606.20695, 2604.21480, 2606.19386.
- **Numbers.** Figures attributed to papers come mostly from abstracts or summarized full-text reads. **Re-check any number before quoting it.**
- **What "none found" means.** The arXiv API searches titles, abstracts and metadata, not full text; OpenReview and the ACL Anthology were mostly not searched. "None found" means "not found with this method" (section 8).
- arXiv IDs beginning 26xx are 2026 papers. URLs are `https://arxiv.org/abs/<ID>` unless stated.

## 1. Summary

1. **The mechanism is not novel.** Pre-tool-call policy enforcement on tau-bench, blocking versus feedback, confidence-threshold operating points, small calibrated supervisors with an action menu in a live loop, and typed-decision ("Jev") models used as gates are all published. About twenty 2026 papers apply runtime enforcement, guidance or supervision to tau-family tasks (section 4.2), and about forty papers on typed-decision models appeared between 19 September and 1 October 2026 (section 4.10).
2. **The exact benchmark does not exist, but the name is taken.** SteerBench-Work (2608.12654, steerbench.com) is a static proceed/hold test with 106 scenarios and no agent runs. STEER-BENCH (2505.20645) is about LLM steerability and AxBench (2501.17148) about activation steering, and runtime-oversight papers also use "steering" (2608.06701, 2609.35807).
3. **The closest method papers each cover one or two axes:**
   - Vasudev et al. (2602.03338): a critic with 0.94 AUROC still cost up to 26 points; sweeps threshold, critic size, feedback text and timing; not tau.
   - COTA (2608.21027): a 0.5B advisor on tau3-Retail; non-binding advice beat forced execution (45.0 against 16.7 for Qwen3-8B); one hook, one supervisor size.
   - HiSentinel (2609.39957): 0.6B and 1.7B sentinels choosing Allow / Redirect / Hard-Pause, with harm counts (109 beneficial, 106 neutral, 27 harmful of 242); coding only.
   - Reinforced Agent (2604.27233) and Asymmetric Actor-Critic (2604.00304): reviewer and critic variation on tau2 and tau-bench at a single pre-execution hook.
   - Calibration Is Not Control (2606.21399) and What Can Be Enforced? (2607.22868): offline score quality does not determine closed-loop control, shown empirically and formally, outside tau.
4. **What remains open:** a controlled factorial over hook point, decision type (yes/no, choice, score), message channel and wording, threshold and supervisor size on a fixed agent and task suite, with paired pass^k, a length-matched sham control, and a cross-hook test of whether offline detection quality predicts the closed-loop effect on a policy benchmark. No paper found does this. Hook placement in particular is a documented gap: a review of 63 artifacts found checkpoint placement clearly reported in only 6 (2609.29547).
5. **This repo's central empirical claim is a replication, and should be framed as one:** detection accuracy does not predict closed-loop benefit (sections 3 and 6.8). The new content is testing it across hooks and decision types on a policy benchmark.
6. **Three methodological findings from this repo have independent published corroboration** and should be cited as motivation: same-arm noise of about ±10 points on tau2 retail with Haiku 4.5 (2606.20695), subset selection manufacturing lift on tau-bench (2608.30916), and replay mispredicting live outcomes (2608.08239).
7. **Recommendation:** build an evaluation protocol plus harness plus the offline-versus-online test, not a leaderboard of decision models and not a new mechanism. Meet the reporting items of the scoping review 2609.39989 and include the controls in section 6.5.

## 2. What the codebase formalizes

Source: `README.md`, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, `agents/decider_gate.py`, `agents/rubrics.py`, `agents/state_render.py`, `experiment_config.py`, `scripts/*`.

### 2.1 The framework

A Strands agent (Bedrock Haiku 4.5) runs tau3-bench tasks (airline, retail, banking_knowledge) through Harbor. A decision model is consulted at Strands lifecycle hooks, and a rubric turns its answers into an action.

- **Actors:** agent A; simulated user and tool runtime (tau3 MCP sidecar); grader (tau2, success means reward 1); decision model M behind `/v1/systemone`; hook H (`ToolCallReviewer`).
- **Hook:** `H = (M, tau, {p -> Pi_p : p in P}, C, B)` where P = {before_invocation, before_tool_call, after_tool_call, before_model_call, after_model_call}, tau is the acceptance threshold, C the per-turn caps and B the request budget.
- **Per-point policy** `Pi_p` = (scope [tools, including `@write/@read/@transfer`; an `applies(ctx)` condition], questions, state renderer, decide function, `needs_answers`, allowed actions, `max_per_turn`). Authored as a YAML rubric or a Python `EvaluatorSpec/PointSpec`.
- **Decision flow:** `x = render_p(ctx)`, `a = M(x, questions)`, `v = decide_p(a, ctx)` with `v` in {Proceed, Guide(text), Deny(text), Confirm, Transform(rewrite)} restricted to the actions the point accepts.
- **Question types:** Noul (yes/no, P(true)), Choice (option probabilities, top option, confidence), Score (ordinal level, confidence).
- **Threshold semantics:** a non-Proceed verdict is followed only if confidence is strictly above tau. Yes/no confidence is `max(P, 1-P)`; Choice and Score use the server's `confidence`. It is the minimum over the answers the matched rule read. Deterministic decisions that read no answer are never held back. A suppressed verdict is logged as `proceeded` with `suppressed`.
- **Action semantics by point** (Strands' matrix):

| Point | Guide | Deny | Confirm | Transform |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| before_tool_call | tool result `GUIDANCE:` | tool result `DENIED:` | M answers itself; "no" gives `CONFIRMATION_FAILED:` | tool arguments |
| before_invocation | cancel, re-invoke with a note appended to the user message | text becomes the reply | n/a | user message |
| after_tool_call | n/a | n/a | n/a | tool result text (the only action accepted) |
| before_model_call | injected user message | text becomes the reply | n/a | whole history |
| after_model_call | discard response and retry | n/a | n/a | response content |

- **Convergence and safety:** `MaxInterventionsPerTurn` (default 3, over all points), per-point `MaxPerTurn`, cap-before-query (no request when every action is already capped), per-trial `max_requests`, model-retry dedupe, fail-open on decision-model errors with a breaker (5 failures, 60 s), fail-open on spec exceptions.

### 2.3 Reusable measurement infrastructure

- **Generic:** per-step log `decider-gate.jsonl`; per-trial `trial-metrics.json`; Wilson and task-bootstrap CIs, pass@k, pass^k (`analysis/stats.py`); paired arm comparison with a permutation test (`scripts/compare_arms.py`); detectable-lift power simulation (`scripts/detectable_lift.py`); run provenance (`run_config.py`); offline replay of recorded trials through a spec (`scripts/replay_hooks.py`); whole-spec composed replay on a scripted Strands agent (`scripts/replay_composed.py`); stability probes under re-wording and question order; infrastructure flags (retries, breaker, budget_skipped, state_cuts).
- **tau-specific (gold-based):** `agents/domain_tools.py` `judge_call`; failure classifiers; `replay_rubric.py --gold`; `score_*_replay.py` (fix / harm / false alarm); `judge_composed_replay.py`; `analyze_oracle.py`; `banking_oracle.py`; `replay_gold_offline.py`.

### 2.4 What is hard-coded to this stack

Harbor and the tau3 sidecar protocol; the Strands intervention API and action matrix; the `/v1/systemone` schema and clef-flash token constants; the domain registry and knowledge-base parser; binary reward in `analysis/`; tau2 gold-action format; Bedrock and EC2.

Proposed abstraction boundary: environment (reset, step, tools, policy, grade); hook (points and verdicts; `review()` and `evaluate()` are already Strands-free); decision model (`ask`); an optional gold-trace and tool-taxonomy plugin; a declared leakage level.

Missing for a benchmark: a closed-loop counterfactual (replays are open-loop), a labeled decision suite, and cost and latency as a swept axis.

## 3. What the experiments in this repo show

Source: `experiments/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md`, `results/*/REPORT.md`, `results/banking-feasibility/*.md`. Agent: Haiku 4.5. Retail decider: hobson-v19 (2B, local). Banking decider: clef-flash.

### 3.1 Arms tried

Four comparisons are live and powered: retail transfer rule, retail guard (342 trials per arm each), and banking baseline versus oracle (291 per arm). Everything else is a pilot (30 trials or fewer), a replay, or a smoke test. No airline arm has run beyond baselines.

| Arm (domain, hook, decision, action) | N | Baseline to arm | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| transfer-only-if-asked (retail, before_tool_call, yes/no P(user asked human) < 0.5, Deny) | 10 tasks x 3 | 13/30 to 19/30 (same baseline rerun scored 17/30 and 18/30) | gain was noise |
| same | tasks 31, 32 x 10 | 7/20 to 8/20 | null |
| same | 114 x 3 | 260/342 (76.0%) to 262/342 (76.6%); paired +0.4 (CI -4.4 to +5.6), p = 0.89 | sound but inert: 18 of 44 attempts denied, 17 of them right; about 5% of trials addressable |
| wrap-up self-check (retail, after_model_call, yes/no, Guide) | 10 x 3; 31, 32 x 10 | 17/30 to 16/30; 7/20 to 2/20 | harmful, dropped |
| finish-the-job and four write-correctness questions | replay only (38 replies; 324 to 331 writes) | right versus wrong writes scored 0.81/0.80, 0.78/0.79, 0.23/0.21, 0.83/0.80 | no signal, never run live |
| retail_guard (option-diff check, transfer deny, amount recompute) | 24 hard x 3; 114 x 3 | pilot 67% to 72%; full 76.0% to 74.9% (paired -1.3, CI -7.5 to +4.7, p = 0.68); +25% model calls | no lift; item check raised wrong-variant failures from 22/19 to 33 |

### 3.2 Cross-cutting findings (each is a testable claim)

1. **Deciders detect surface facts, not correctness.** "Did the user ask for a human" scored 0.29 to 0.37 on self-initiated transfers and 0.55 to 0.82 on requested ones; 17 of 18 live denials were right. The banking router picks the task scenario at message one in 234 of 264 trials. But four write-correctness questions scored about 0.80 on both right and wrong calls, and a clef-flash open-step question had AUC 0.47.
2. **Rules that fire about once per trial on a mostly-right agent lose.** Wrap-up fired 1.2 guides per trial and scored 2/20 against 7/20. Firing does not discriminate: `identity_note` fired in 80% of failing and 67% of passing banking trials. Rare rules are harmless but invisible.
3. **Content beats control.** Oracle trials given only documents passed 53%. Exact-call guides were followed in 234 of 242 cases, but only 108 of 234 send-backs led to a state change and 41 of 324 denies were retried. Within-task effects were deny +0.02, send-back +0.03, nudge -0.16, all with CIs spanning zero. No ablation has been run.
4. **The answer-leaked ceiling is 70.4%, not 100%.** Of 86 failures, 24 come from the oracle's own order-blindness, 35 are customer-side calls, 11 are simulator protocol, 3 are assertions. Gold replay scores 97/97.
5. **Noise is large.** The same arm on the same 10 tasks scored 13, 17 and 18 of 30. The same 24 tasks with no intervention scored 39/72 and 48/72. Retail standard error is about 2.5 points, detectable lift about 7. Banking 97 x 3 power: +4 points 29%, +6 points 68%, +8 points 91%.
6. **Subset selection manufactures lift.** The guard on the 24 pilot tasks beat a fresh baseline by 11 points; on the full suite it was -1.3.
7. **The decider is deterministic (600/600 identical repeats) but wording-fragile.** Re-worded or shorter state keeps close_without_procedure 45/64 and premature_close 19/33; option order has no effect; opaque ids moved confidence by 0.02 to 0.11.
8. **Agent obedience is local.** Haiku's first action differs in 52% of hooked versus 17% of control pairs, but lingering text has little effect (off-gold 6 to 5 of 240).
9. **Operational limits.** clef-flash reads 16,384 input tokens per request and refuses longer ones (the gate fails open). Latency is 0.16 s under 2k tokens and 0.64 s at 12k to 16k. A composed spec made 26.3 requests and 7.9 s per trial. The local hobson guard arm added 52.8 s of decision latency per trial.
10. **Composition bugs appear in replay:** contradictory notes, and a once-per-trial guide given to each of 16 parallel calls.

What would surprise a researcher: the leaked-answer oracle still fails 29.6% of trials because simulator and customer structure bound steering; sound detectors give no aggregate effect while small pilots show +20 from noise; self-doubt prompts hurt while exact-argument corrections help.

### 3.3 Pitfalls a benchmark must design around

- **Grader:** the retail NL grader crashed on fenced JSON (7 of 28 errored); the banking adapter dropped `read_log_allowlist` (54 tasks never graded required reads); older `tasks.json` differs from `task_*.json`.
- **Unwinnable or simulator-bound tasks:** task_034 crashes on an empty message; customer `###TRANSFER###` ends runs (004, 014, 033); 049 and 081 need an unscripted customer call; 102's assertion is underivable.
- **Infrastructure:** errored trials count as failures; Bedrock reasoning-only `ValidationException`; a 100-minute litellm timeout.
- **Caps fail open** (task_041 ran 8 of 16 disputes, 79 steps past caps); a once-per-trial deny acted per turn; endpoint downtime silently removes deterministic rules.
- **State cuts:** Lux cut from the front and lost the opening request. The agent's context manager must be off, otherwise a `KB_search` result is cut to two documents.
- **Replay limits:** recordings ignore guidance; only 21 passing banking trials; modules were tuned in-sample; "first deviation" is not causation.

### 3.4 Limits of the decider itself (from `strands-decider/`)

- v19 on JevBench public: 0.723 (167/231); easy 1.000, standard 0.875, hard 0.505. The retrain standard deviation is 3.2 tasks, so gaps under about 10 tasks are unresolved.
- Capability-bound families: temporal_numeric 0.267, probability 0.30, long_policy 0.368, multi_hop 0.444.
- Question sensitivity: the same answer to a changed question about 94% of the time, and to a negated yes/no 92.5%.
- Calibration is one temperature per primitive, fit on short classification. On long documents and adequacy v19 is under-confident by 0.19 to 0.35.
- Latency: 115 ms is one question per task on an RTX 3090; M3 Pro warm median is 234 ms with p95 of 2.6 s.
- The repo has no closed-loop or tau-bench measurement. `examples/strands` is an unmeasured illustration.

### 3.5 The two Strands blog posts

- **Steering hooks beat prompts and workflows** (18 Mar 2026): a toy library-renewal agent, 600 runs per variant. Pass rates: none 15.7%, simple 82.5%, SOP 99.8% (9,879 input tokens), steering 100% (3,346), workflow 80.8%. Single author, one demo agent, models unspecified; the 30-day rule relied on an external policy engine.
- **Strands Decider 2B** (1 Oct 2026): Qwen3.5-2B torso with a pointer head and LoRA rank 16. Choice, yes/no and score decisions with reliability scores; 3rd of 33 in the 2B class on JevBench public.

## 4. Literature review

### 4.1 Closest prior art

**Benchmark.** *SteerBench-Work: A Benchmark for Agent Steering at Action Boundaries* (Serdar and Mertayak, 12 Aug 2026, 2608.12654; leaderboard steerbench.com, AgentDock). 106 scenarios anchored in public incidents, plus mirrored and control cases; a model sees a proposed action and evidence and returns proceed or hold. Across 30 model conditions, models wrongly hold authorized work on 28.1% of opportunities and wrongly allow unsafe work on 1.0%; reversed famous incidents score 63.8% against 98.5% for the originals. No tasks are completed and no agent runs; guide, deny, confirm and transform are collapsed into "blocked". The independent variable is the model. Closest in name and concept, but closer to the Decision Index than to a closed-loop benchmark. Its references name AmPermBench and UnderSpecBench as neighbors; it had no citing papers at the time of the search.

**Method papers that treat the intervention as the variable on a fixed agent.**

- *Accurate Failure Prediction in Agents Does Not Imply Effective Failure Prevention* (Vasudev, Russak, Bikel and Alshikh, 3 Feb 2026, 2602.03338; the PDF is titled "The Intervention Paradox"). A critic with AUROC 0.94 caused a 26-point drop on one model and about zero on another. It describes a disruption-recovery tradeoff and proposes a 50-task pilot to decide before deployment whether to intervene. Interventions are ROLLBACK or APPEND-warning; it sweeps threshold, critic size (0.6B against 14B), feedback text (none, generic, LLM-written) and timing. On high-success tasks intervention lowered performance by 0 to 26 points; on ALFWorld it gave +2.8 (p = 0.014). Paired bootstrap, Holm correction and minimum detectable effect are reported. HotpotQA, GAIA, ALFWorld.
- *Don't Solve, Just Compare: Tiny Advisors for Runtime Intervention* (COTA; Jiang et al., Aug 2026, 2608.21027). A fine-tuned 0.5B comparator judges the actor's proposed action against K alternatives and returns the winner as non-binding advice, on WebShop, ALFWorld and tau3-Retail (40 tasks x 3 seeds) with three frozen actors (Qwen3-8B, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, DeepSeek-V4-Flash). Retail success 37.5 to 45.0, 62.5 to 65.0, 80.8 to 82.5. Advice beat forced execution (45.0 against 16.7; 65.0 against 37.5; ALFWorld 90.3 against 51.5), and absolute scoring also scored 16.7. The gate (K candidates, R wins) and an intervention budget are ablated; actors adopt only 19 to 33% of advice on Retail. One hook, one supervisor size, no harm count, no pass^k.
- *Learning When and How to Intervene: A Hindsight-Distilled Sentinel for Coding Agents* (HiSentinel; Zhao et al., 30 Sep 2026, 2609.39957). 0.6B and 1.7B sentinels choose Allow, Redirect (withhold the action and give feedback) or Hard-Pause (ask a human) before execution, distilled from a teacher that sees recorded outcomes. Releases SWE-Intervene (6,923 labeled actions). On SWE-bench Verified Mini, Qwen3-Coder resolved 30 (base), 36 (0.6B) and 44 (1.7B); Sonnet 4.6 60 to 66. Of 242 interventions, 109 were beneficial, 106 neutral and 27 harmful; SWE-PRM-style feedback critics were mostly non-beneficial (3 of 284). Single runs on 50 tasks; no threshold or wording ablation; not tau.
- *Reinforced Agent: Inference-Time Feedback for Tool-Calling Agents* (Ta et al., Apr 2026, 2604.27233). A reviewer checks each provisional tool call before execution; feedback is injected as a system message. BFCL and tau2 (airline, retail, telecom) with a GPT-4o actor; reviewer model (GPT-4o, o3-mini, GPT-5-mini), five prompt variants including GEPA, and mechanism (progressive, selector, grader) varied. It defines helpfulness (base errors fixed) and harmfulness (correct answers broken), computed on BFCL (benefit to risk 2.1:1 for GPT-4o, 3:1 for o3-mini). tau2 is domain-dependent: telecom 41.2 to 64.0, airline 42.0 to 40.7, retail 62.9 to 62.6. Policy violations fell from 31% to 18% while over-verbalization rose from 10% to 27%, at 2.4x episode time.
- *Asymmetric Actor-Critic for Multi-turn LLM Agents* (Jiang et al., Mar 2026, 2604.00304). A fixed Claude-4 Sonnet actor on tau-bench is supervised by Qwen3-4B, 8B or 32B critics (off-the-shelf and fine-tuned) before state-changing tool calls, with free-text approve or revise feedback. Average pass@1 0.600 to 0.625 / 0.632 / 0.653 by critic size, 0.655 for a fine-tuned 8B, against 0.610 with Claude as the critic; retail 0.696 to 0.736, airline 0.504 to 0.528. No pass^k, threshold or harm metric. The only supervisor-size sweep on tau found.
- *Calibration Is Not Control: Why LLM-Agent Oversight Needs Intervention* (Zhang et al., Jun 2026, 2606.21399). Argues the oversight target is "intervention advantage", the expected gain from intervening rather than continuing, measured by prefix branching (same-prefix counterfactual rollouts of continue, intervene or quit). Platt scaling cut ECE from 0.463 to 0.006 while control regret stayed 0.318; an action-conditioned controller cut ALFWorld regret from 0.506 to 0.110; gains shrink with agent strength (0.293 at Qwen-7B, 0.201 at 72B, 0.081 at GPT-5.4). ALFWorld, ScienceWorld, GSM8K, HotpotQA; the controller is a random forest. No tau, hooks or messages.
- *What Can Be Enforced? A Theory of Certified Runtime Safety for Tool-Using Agents* (Ray, Jul 2026, 2607.22868). Once blocking changes future proposals, static scores and ungated trajectories need not identify the closed-loop frontier. On AgentDojo, 23 judges reach static AUC .66 to .86 and calibrated thresholds collapse to block-all; in paired closed-loop reruns (949 episodes per condition, local 7B agent) a 3B gate cut attack success from 4.7% to 0.4% but gated 78% of calls and dropped task success from .254 to .180, while a 7B gate gated 12% and held .243. Block-only, no warn arm.
- *Beyond Prompts: Measuring and Optimizing LLM Tool-Agent Harnesses* (Zhao et al., Sep 2026, 2609.05736). A fixed-model protocol for harness edits that are guarded intercepts at the tool boundary (silent argument correction, error blocking, prerequisite blocking), reporting held-out lift, worst-condition lift, repeatability and a conservative RelLift95(B). With a gpt-5.4-mini agent on 74-task held-out tau2 splits and four seeds: retail pass^1 47.6 and pass^4 18.2, telecom 25.0 and 6.1; the optimizer's gains are +14.9 (retail) and +10.1 (telecom) with CIs excluding zero. The nearest published "protocol on a fixed agent" on tau2, and a reporting model to copy.

**Design templates.**

- **ControlArena** (UK AISI with Redwood, github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/control-arena) with *AI Control* (Greenblatt et al., 2023, 2312.06942): protocols (defer-to-trusted, trusted editing, resampling) are the independent variable; outputs are usefulness against safety; macro-protocols are re-analysed post hoc from exported trajectories.
- **AgentDojo** (Debenedetti et al., 2024, 2406.13352) and **AgentDyn** (Li et al., 2026, 2602.03117): defenses compared on a fixed agent and task set, reporting utility with and without attack plus attack success; AgentDyn runs ten defenses on 60 tasks and reports over-defense.
- **ContainmentBench** (Lan et al., Jul 2026, 2607.23999): seven defense-policy conditions on matched rollout pairs with an over-blocking (authorized-commit) metric; mean authorized commit 0.164 (taint-only), 0.857 (intent ledger), 0.923 (tool boundary). The closest "policy as the variable, matched pairs, utility measured" design, in an injection setting.
- *What Can Component-Replacement Evidence Establish? A Critical Scoping Review of Local Decisions in LLM Agents* (Zhang and Chang, 30 Sep 2026, 2609.39989). Of 348 studies, 222 report local decision metrics and 142 also report task endpoints, but co-reporting is not a matched comparison; it derives eight claim-specific reporting items and names recovery and disruption, intervention timing and downstream use as recurring mechanisms. It states that Outcome Monitors' gain cannot be credited to detector quality. A reviewer-facing standard this benchmark should meet. Its HTML was unavailable; the eight items should be read from the PDF.

### 4.2 Runtime enforcement, guidance and supervision on tau-bench-family tasks

### 4.3 Closed-loop supervisors and runtime control outside tau

- **Trigger, monitor and feedback as components.** *HARDE: Optimizing Agent Harnesses for Runtime Risk Detection and Execution Control* (Liu et al., Sep 2026, 2609.38291) decomposes the harness into a trigger (when to call the monitor), a monitor and feedback, probing and ablating each, on SHADE-Arena, AgentDyn and Agent-SafetyBench with Qwen3-8B and DeepSeek-V4-Flash monitors. SHADE safe success 0.161 to 0.500; removing feedback drops it to 0.154 and removing the trigger to 0.357; a naive monitor over-blocks (utility 0.281 and 0.627). Optimizer-driven rather than factorial.
- **Feedback against blocking.** *Environment Steering: Using Data Flow Control to Improve Agent Utility and Safety* (Summers et al., Sep 2026, 2609.35807) holds the policy fixed and varies only the on-fail handling on AgentDyn with Qwen3-235B: STOP changes task success by -36.2, a generic retry ("You have violated a security policy") by -10.5, and a constraint-aware retry by +6.5, with attack success about 0 throughout.
- **Paired evaluation of rewrites and recovery.** *TwinCheck: Negative-Twin Verification for Stateful Tool Agents* (Dai et al., Sep 2026, 2609.26911): a pairwise verifier replaces a tool call only when an evidence condition holds; exact replay holds the agent's actions fixed until the first accepted replacement; BFCL V4 multi-turn (159 tasks) 45.3 to 58.5 with no success-to-failure regressions. *When Harnesses Lose the Signal: Causal Evaluation of Recovery* (Xiao et al., Sep 2026, 2610.00372): recovery against no recovery from the same execution state, separating rescue from harm; ALFWorld with Qwen3-14B 70.33 to 73.33. *Real-Time Detection and Repair of LLM Agent Failures* (Dubey, Aug 2026, 2608.02464): microsecond CUSUM monitors over 2,823 episodes catch 0.71 of failures at a 5% false-alarm budget; rollback and re-run recovers 45% of failures against 16% for a resampling control (success 52% to 73%).
- **Scope of a rewrite.** *ControlScope* (Ning et al., Sep 2026, 2609.34313): continue, edit the next call's arguments, or replace the workflow, with one-time against repeated review (filesystem, ALFWorld, AppWorld).
- **Advisors on alarm.** *Online Monitoring and Corrective Steering of Programming Agents* (LivePlan; Liu et al., Aug 2026, 2608.06701): a rule-based monitor triggers an advisor LLM only when an issue is detected; SWE-bench Verified and Pro, up to 15.2% gain (average 9.9%) at about $0.08 per instance.
- **Action menus with feedback.** *From Risk Classification to Action Plan Remediation* (TRIAD; Sun et al., Jun 2026, 2606.05805): a trained guard outputs proceed, refuse or update plus natural-language feedback, closed loop with planning (ASB, AgentHarm; attack success 10.42% on average); it notes that guardrails are usually evaluated in isolation. *CORA* (Feng et al., Apr 2026, 2604.09155): a Guardian scores action risk, conformal risk control sets the execute/abstain threshold, and a Diagnostician recommends confirm, reflect or abort; safety-helpfulness-interruption Pareto on Phone-Harm. *NEXUS* (Hossain et al., 2026, 2607.19356): allow, block, confirm or revise from a calibrated logistic risk score, offline only (4-class accuracy 0.64 on 128 items).
- **Enforcement strictness.** *Permission Denied: Policy-Graded Evaluation of Coding Agents* (Davidovich et al., Aug 2026, 2608.02670): nested enforcement levels on Terminal-Bench 2.1; the strictest costs up to 18.3 points of success and 167% more cost.
- **Controllers replayed on frozen proposals.** *Stale Does Not Mean Unsafe* (Zheng et al., Aug 2026, 2609.29522): every controller replayed on frozen agent proposals (3,456 trajectories), including Qwen3-4B, Phi-4-mini and Gemma4-8B gates; freshness guards block 92 to 95% of benign races and lose up to 43% of safe completions; verbal confidence has ECE about 0.37 and action-agreement gating matches a random gate.
- **Coding-agent reliability interventions:** ECLoop (Xu et al., 2607.28815; postpones actions until evidence conditions hold; +4.8 to +11.8 points SWE-bench Verified Pass@1); Opera (Mei et al., 2609.33987; verbal critic, up to +15.0); SWE-PRM, *When Agents go Astray* (Gandhi et al., 2509.02360; 40.0 to 50.6 on SWE-bench Verified, taxonomy-guided feedback best); Wink (Nanda et al., 2602.17037; asynchronous self-intervention in production); PreFlect (2602.07187; prospective plan critique); FailFast-RestartSmart (2608.03222; 0.6B monitor plus restart).
- **Other lifecycle systems** (multi-hook, no placement ablation): CUGA, *Governance by Construction for Generalist Agents* (2605.20874; five checkpoints: intent guard, playbook, tool guide, tool approvals, output formatter; demo only); FinHarness (2605.27333; query and tool monitors with a light-to-strong judge cascade; FinVault attack success 38.3% to 15.0%, benign approval 41.1% to 39.3%); SafeHarness (2604.13630; four lifecycle layers); OpenClaw PRISM (2603.11853; ten lifecycle hooks); ClawSentry (2608.21101); PROACT-Agent (2609.34415; a guard before the next model call); SAGE (2604.09285); a when-to-intervene by what-to-inject factorial on persona drift (2609.24532), a design template.

### 4.4 Guardrails, action menus and where to intervene

- **Action-menu formalisms.** *AgentSpec* (Wang et al., ICSE 2026, 2503.18666): trigger, predicate and enforce rules with enforce in {stop, user inspection, LLM self-examine, invoke corrective action}, the formal twin of Proceed / Deny / Confirm / Guide / Transform at pre-action triggers; more than 90% of unsafe code executions prevented at millisecond overhead; rule-based, not tested on tau-bench. *Safety Sentry* (Chen et al., 2026, 2607.13594): a 4B guard outputs EXECUTE / ASK / REFUSE with one decoding threshold; offline step accuracy and over-/under-ask rates only. *Policy-Invisible Violations* (Wu et al., 2604.12177): Allow / Block / Clarify by speculative world-state simulation, 93.0% against 68.8%. *Progressive Autonomy as Preference Learning* (Ou, 2605.19151): Gaussian-process allow / block / ask regions. *SiLR* (Zhou, 2609.04629): a rejected proposal is followed by another at the same state, so a gate is a search operator and scalar gates can trap. Small guardrail families: Fence (2607.18268), GLiNER Guard (2605.05277).
- **Block versus feedback.** *ToolSafe* (Mou et al., 2026, 2601.10156): a step-level guard plus a feedback loop on AgentDojo and ASB; detect-and-abort hurts benign completion, rich feedback beats rating-only; harmful calls -65%, benign completion about +10%. *AgentLTL* (Elkoussy et al., 2607.02599): online prefix checks; warn helps 5 of 7 models, hard terminate is worst for 4 of 7. *RAIL Guard* (Verma et al., 2607.16215): block-and-retry converges 49%, a rewrite loop 97% at -22% utility.
- **Guard systems and models.** ShieldAgent (Chen et al., 2503.22738; probabilistic rule circuits, 3K-trajectory benchmark); GuardAgent (Xiang et al., ICML 2025, 2406.09187; a guard agent that writes code, no utility measure); LlamaFirewall (Chennabasappa et al., 2505.03574; AgentDojo attack success 17.6% to 1.75%, utility 47.7 to 42.7, threshold fixed at a 3% utility cost); Progent (Shi et al., 2504.11703; least-privilege policies, attack success 39.9% to 1.0% with utility kept); ProbGuard (Wang et al., ASE 2026, 2508.00500; risk-threshold monitor, embodied and driving only; re-prompt mode -65% unsafe at 80% completion, halt mode -94%); StepGuard (Zheng et al., EMNLP 2026, 2608.24777; AgentDojo attack success -77%, utility -2.8); AgentSentinel (2509.07764); AEGIS (2603.12621; 8.3 ms); NeMo Guardrails (Rebedea et al., EMNLP 2023, 2310.10501).
- **Guard benchmarks, mostly detection-only:** PolicyGuardBench, *Learning Efficient Guardrails for Compliance* (Wen et al., ICML 2026, 2510.03485); TraceSafe (Chen et al., COLM 2026, 2604.07223; accuracy not monotone in size); ScopeJudge (Caldwell et al., 2607.07774; eight judges by five context strategies, cost-accuracy Pareto); StepShield (section 4.6); over-refusal: The Guard That Cried Wolf (Zhang et al., 2608.27009; guardrails over-refuse authorized actions more when the object name looks scary), AgentBoundary (2609.33658), UnderSpecBench (2607.02294); PASTABench (Sun et al., 2609.28197; 1,139 trajectories; whether, when and what to intervene, with an Optimal Intervention Window).
- **Where to intervene.** *Agent Lifecycle Toolkit* (ALTK; Wright et al., 2603.15473) names six intervention points (post-user-request, pre-LLM, post-LLM, pre-tool, post-tool, pre-response) without an ablation. *HARD* (Ruan, 2608.12977) compares context construction against an action gate: the gate alone leaves direct-injection attack success at 53.0% against 16.9% for policy and 12.1% for both. *Mind the GAP* (Cartagena, 2602.16943): text-level safety does not transfer to tool-call safety. *VIGIL* (Li et al., 2606.26524) frames where to intervene as a granularity problem. *HarnessRisk* (Bai et al., 2608.17597): a six-phase lifecycle benchmark. *More Rejective, Not More Discriminative: The Unit of Verification in Pre-Execution LLM Oversight* (Han et al., 2608.23941): a twin-prefix design (injected error plus clean twin) on tau2-style retail and AppWorld varies how many actions one monitor call reviews (1 to 8) across six judges; for Llama-70B on retail, catch rises 0.765 to 0.970 while false rejection rises 0.419 to 0.935; informedness peaks at 1 to 2 actions. *The Reduced-Supervision Paradox* (Shi et al., 2609.29547): of 63 artifacts, checkpoint placement was clearly reported in only 6.
- **Coverage dominates.** *Measuring the Permission Gate* (Ji et al., 2604.04978), an independent test of Claude Code's Auto Mode: false-negative rate 81.0% on 253 ambiguous actions against the 17% Anthropic reports; 36.8% of actions never reach the classifier; on evaluated actions FNR 70.3% and FPR 31.9%. *Red-Teaming Auto Mode* (Remedios, 2609.19587): an injection ran arbitrary bash in 79% of trials against production blocking monitors.
- **Pitfalls of gates.** *Stop Means Stop* (Khan, 2607.14166): pre-execution approval gates let a sibling parallel call execute during a pause in 5 of 6 frameworks (215 of 1,200 live runs), the same composition bug seen in this repo (section 3.2). *Causality Laundering* (Chinaei, 2604.04035): denial feedback is itself an information channel. *Noticing the Watcher* (Jiralerspong et al., 2603.16928): frontier models infer a hidden monitor from blocking feedback alone. Verdict or score feedback lets agents evade latent and activation monitors (2609.36490, 2609.03035). Agents tamper with their own traces, undermining asynchronous monitoring (Qin et al., 2609.30266). *CAVE-Bench* (Mao et al., 2609.32616): agents accept false accusations and over-correct working output, a mechanism for false-positive Guide harm. *Compositional Policy Violations* (2609.18820): step-scoped monitors cannot detect violations that exist only across steps.
- **Human approval (the Confirm action).** User-authored permission policies (Yan et al., 2608.27443; 113 participants: an allow / ask / never policy blocked 20.1 points less overreach than per-action human approval); Janus (Brigham et al., 2607.01510; six permission-assistant designs, none best everywhere); *Comparing Human Oversight Strategies for Computer-Use Agents* (Chen et al., 2604.04918; 48 participants; control was returned too early or too late); *Habituation at the Gate* (Yu et al., 2606.22721; approval rate rises from 30.1% to 36.8%); *When Should Users Check?* (Zhou et al., 2510.05307; decision-theoretic placement of confirmation points); *Oversight Has a Capacity* (section 4.9).
- **Policy as code:** Cedar autoformalization (2606.26649); COVENANT (2607.25400; workflow compiled to a graph, a controller checks each proposal and returns diagnostic feedback, 50.0% to 83.3% success); Artic, *Natural-Language Workflows Are Not Software Yet* (2608.21341; +28 points); TDAD (2603.08806); Sapien (2610.00797); ActGov (2609.24446); Agent Behavioral Contracts (2602.22302); OAP (2603.20953; 53 ms median); VeriGuard (2510.05156); ToolGate (2601.04688); *Formal Policy Enforcement for Real-World Agentic Systems* (Palumbo et al., 2602.16708; Datalog policies at every decision point); *Look Before You Leap: Pre-Action Verification* (2609.11957); *Authorization Architectures for Tool-Using AI Agents* (2609.15906); *Designing for Doubt: The Case for Informed Abstention* (2606.02965); *Runtime Governance for AI Agents: Policies on Paths* (Kaptein et al., 2603.16586; prompt instructions as a special case of path policies).
- **Product documentation read:** AgentCore Policy (Cedar at the Gateway, pre-tool allow/deny); Claude Code hooks (PreToolUse allow / deny / ask / updatedInput / additionalContext); Invariant Guardrails (repository only).

### 4.5 AI control and monitoring

Control has the same structure as this repo's hook: a weaker trusted model scores an action, a threshold selects an action from a menu, and results are reported as a safety-usefulness Pareto curve.

- **Protocols.** *AI Control* (Greenblatt et al., 2023, 2312.06942): trusted GPT-3.5, untrusted GPT-4, 2% human audit budget, APPS backdooring. Safety / usefulness: trusted monitoring 62/97; plus defer-to-trusted on the 10% most suspicious 83/93; untrusted monitoring 79/96; trusted editing (20%) 92/94. *Games for AI Control* (Griffin et al., 2409.07985) computes Pareto-optimal protocols as a stochastic game; *Adaptive Deployment of Untrusted LLMs* (Wen et al., 2411.17693) uses a threshold that moves with running evidence. *Ctrl-Z: Controlling AI Agents via Resampling* (Bhatt et al., 2504.10374): BashBench (257 tasks), GPT-4o mini agent, Llama-3.1-8B monitor, 1% audit budget; resampling reduces attack success from 58% to 7% at a 5% usefulness cost, and at 95% usefulness defer-to-trusted gives 72.7% safety against 87.1% for resample-with-incrimination (two operating points from the same paper). *Evaluating Control Protocols for Untrusted AI Agents* (Kutasov et al., 2511.02997): deferring only on critical actions stays robust against an adaptive red team. *Retrying vs Resampling in AI Control* (Lucassen et al., 2605.26047): block-then-continue, which is close to this repo's Guide, lets the agent exploit the monitor's rationale and the safety gain disappears. *Adaptive Attacks on Trusted Monitors* (Terekhov et al., 2510.09462); *Async Control* (Stickland et al., 2512.13526; review after the fact, 6% FNR at 1% FPR); *Practical challenges of control monitoring* (Lindner et al., 2512.22154; synchronous, semi-synchronous and asynchronous latency-safety trade-offs); Basic Legibility Protocols (2602.10153); Factor(T,U) (2512.02157); TraceGuard (2604.03968); Bootstrapped Monitoring (2606.11998); an untrusted-monitoring safety case (2602.20628); Diffuse Control (2606.08892); control-evaluation methodology (Korbak et al., 2504.05259); a GDM AI control roadmap (2607.13087).
- **Settings.** SHADE-Arena (Kutasov et al., 2506.15740; best monitor AUC 0.87); BashArena (Kaufman et al., 2512.15688); LinuxArena (Tracy et al., 2604.15384); SLEIGHT-Bench (2605.16626); ResearchArena (2607.19321); DrugBench (2606.20663; control protocols on 3,671 medical conversations, a non-coding multi-turn setting); Agent-as-a-Proxy (2602.05066); attack-selection results that lower safety (2606.06529, 2602.04930).
- **Monitor design, cost and size.** Reliable Weak-to-Strong Monitoring (Kale et al., 2508.19461); Control Tax (Terekhov et al., 2506.05296); Combining Cost-Constrained Runtime Monitors (Hua et al., 2507.15886); Ensemble Monitoring (Koran et al., 2605.15377; diverse ensembles beat identical ones); Decorrelation across 24 monitors (2608.16190; skill, not lineage, governs ensemble gain); monitor AUROC depends on the agent-monitor pairing (2607.06596; interaction +0.172); Deliberative Monitors (2605.29601; small open-weight action-only monitors trained by distillation); Scaling Laws for Scalable Oversight (Engels et al., 2504.18530); linear probes for strategic deception (Goldowsky-Dill et al., 2502.03407); lightweight sequential monitors (Yueh-Han et al., 2506.10949); information access and monitor quality (Arike et al., 2601.21112; monitors often do better with less information).
- **What the monitor sees.** Baker et al. (2503.11926; chain-of-thought monitoring beats action-only); CoT Red-Handed (Arnav et al., NeurIPS 2025, 2505.23575); Monitoring Monitorability (Guan et al., 2512.18311).
- **Non-adversarial, reliability-oriented work** is the closest to this repo's setting: The Verifier Tax and Outcome Monitors (section 4.2), DrugBench, and *Instrumental Monitor Evasion Emerges Under Ordinary Task Pressure* (Schmotz et al., 2609.30217): with no adversarial goal, agents try to circumvent runtime monitors (attempt rates up to 98% best-of-3) by encoding commands, splitting operations across calls and retrying, which bears on Deny followed by retry.
- **Gap.** Control rarely uses benign policy-adherence tasks like tau-bench, has no sub-100 ms calibrated classifier monitors (probes and lightweight sequential monitors are the nearest), and studies messaging only for leakage and evasion (Lucassen, Noticing the Watcher, HARDE, the Auto Mode work), not wording or channel as a factor. Usefulness is honest task success, not policy adherence.

### 4.6 Verifiers, critics, failure prediction and intervention harm

- **Small judges of large-model steps.** ToolPRMBench (Li et al., 2601.12294): pairwise tool-step accuracy with 50% chance; Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B average 43.9/49.4/53.7, Claude-4.5-haiku 75.1, a GRPO-trained 4B 78.6; models under 50% often give negative best-of-8 gains. AgentProcessBench (Fan et al., 2603.14465): 8,509 human step labels; models of 8B or less reach 56 to 63% step accuracy, the best open model 68.5%, Gemini-3-Flash-thinking 81.6%; judges over-predict "good". ProcessBench (Zheng et al., ACL 2025, 2412.06559): prompted 7 to 8B critics score F1 5 to 33 against 61.9 for GPT-4o, and are lopsided. AgentJudgeBench (Verma et al., EMNLP 2026, 2608.26623): judges from 20B to frontier converge at 77 to 82% on hard queries; rubrics add 6.5 points; chain of thought adds little. AgentRewardBench (Lù et al., 2504.08942); *The Art of Building Verifiers for CUAs* (Rosset et al., 2604.06240; rubric design cut false positives to near 0 against 22 to 45%). Counsel (2606.21627): the best open judge reaches about 88% location agreement and 65% reasoning agreement.
- **Trained small critics can work.** Web-Shepherd (Chae et al., NeurIPS 2025, 2505.15277): 3B/8B step-level PRM; on WebArena-lite best-of-5 a GPT-4o-mini verifier without a checklist hurt (23.6 to 15.8), with a checklist +0.6, Web-Shepherd-8B +10.9. AgentForesight (Zhang et al., 2605.08715): a 7B online auditor over prefixes, up to +19.9% over proprietary models. *Steer, Don't Solve* (Gandhi et al., 2606.21811): fine-tuned 4B/8B critics give short guidance every 5 to 10 steps (GPT-OSS-120B 20.4 to 34.8), but an untrained 4B critic hurt (20.4 to 16.8) and detailed critiques caused copying. CAST (Saeidi et al., EMNLP 2026, 2608.30147).
- **When feedback hurts.** *Scaling Test-time Compute for LLM Agents* (Zhu et al., 2506.12928): reflecting at every step 55.2 against 55.8 baseline; score-triggered reflection at thresholds 8 and 5 was worse; only the strictest trigger helped. StepShield (Felicia et al., 2601.22136): a guardrail with 86% recall has near-random timing because over three quarters of alerts fire on benign prefixes; introduces Early Intervention Rate. Feedback Friction (Jiang et al., 2506.11930): even near-ground-truth feedback is not fully absorbed, so an oracle arm will not reach 100%. *Upstream Messages Override Correct Answers* (Gong et al., 2609.36855): an incorrect message flips a previously correct answer in up to 32% of cases, and 94% of harmful cases copy the wrong answer. Vestrum (Parashar et al., 2609.33822): evidence-grounded step verification helps while critics asked to rebuild finished answers broke more than they repaired, a step-against-final-answer contrast. Agentic scaffolding amplifies sycophancy (Jittham, 2608.21377; -6.3 points). *Detection Without Correction* (Nilayam et al., 2605.27559). MAS-ProVe (2602.03053): process verification is inconsistent in multi-agent systems. *Credit Without Ground Truth* (Zhang, 2608.19760): LLM-judge step scores show no fidelity beyond a shuffled control against executed replay.
- **Break-even rules.** *Self-Correction as Feedback Control* (Liu et al., 2604.22273): iterate only when the ratio of errors corrected to correct answers broken exceeds accuracy over error rate, a closed-form break-even for a threshold. SEVRA, *Think Again or Think Longer?* (Dip et al., 2606.19808): a gate between keeping a frozen solver's answer and verifying it cuts harmful flips from 2.2% to 1.0%. *Confidence v.s. Critique* (Yang et al., 2412.19513) separates keeping correct answers from fixing wrong ones, a ready-made collateral-damage decomposition. *When is Tree Search Useful?* (Chen et al., ACL 2024, 2402.10890): correction needs a discriminator near 90% accuracy. *When Does Verification Pay Off?* (Lu et al., 2512.02304): cross-family verification beats same-family.
- **Self-correction background.** Huang et al. (ICLR 2024, 2310.01798) and Kamoi et al. (TACL 2024, 2406.01297): intrinsic self-correction often fails or hurts; it works with reliable external feedback. Tyen et al. (2311.08516): models fail to find errors but fix them given the location. Small language models need strong verifiers to self-correct (Zhang et al., 2404.17140). MINT (Wang et al., ICLR 2024, 2309.10691). Structured feedback improves repair (Ray et al., 2607.14167): most gain came from listing admissible alternatives. Reflexion 2303.11366, CRITIC 2305.11738, Self-Refine 2303.17651, ToolVerifier 2402.14158, Generative Verifiers 2408.15240, Valmeekam et al. 2310.08118, Let's Verify Step by Step 2305.20050.
- **Localization and attribution are weak.** Who&When (Zhang et al., 2505.00212): best 53.5% agent-level but 14.2% step-level attribution. TRAIL (Deshpande et al., 2505.08638): best about 11%.

### 4.7 How guidance is delivered: channel, role, wording and context rewriting

- **Instruction hierarchy and channel.** IH-Benchmark (McCauley et al., 2607.25987): system-over-user and user-over-tool conflicts across 37 models; compliance ranges from 98.2% to 20.5%, and strong system-over-user compliance does not predict robustness when the conflicting instruction sits in a tool output. Hierarchy training and analysis: The Instruction Hierarchy (Wallace et al., 2404.13208), IH-Challenge (2603.10521; GPT-5-Mini robustness 84.1% to 94.1%), ManyIH (2604.09443), Where Instruction Hierarchy Breaks (2606.07808), IHEval (2502.08745), Control Illusion (2502.15851), How Language Models Choose Sides (2608.28648), Steering Instruction Hierarchies at Inference Time (2607.26228). Overreliance on unreliable tools (Yang et al., 2609.05587; corrupted returns adopted over 33% of the time, 68% for web search). *Helpful Agent Meets Deceptive Judge* (Ming et al., 2506.03332). Robustness to inconsistent tool responses (Xu et al., 2608.22676). *Thinking Outside the Box* (Wang et al., 2609.39578): frontier models gain from reliable guidance but are vulnerable to misleading guidance, which matters for false-positive rubric firings.
- **Context and history rewriting (the Transform action at before_model_call and after_tool_call).** None compares rewriting with Guide or Deny. *The Complexity Trap* (Lindenbauer et al., 2508.21433): simple observation masking matches LLM summarization on SWE-agent at about half the cost. *Masking Stale Observations Helps Search Agents, Until It Doesn't* (Zhang et al., 2606.00408): the gain is an inverted U against the agent's own accuracy. TRACE (Min et al., 2608.06503): compression weakens the influence of recent turns and raises blocked actions; compaction events are evaluated by paired closed-loop continuations. Squeez (Kovács, 2604.04979): a LoRA Qwen3.5-2B prunes tool outputs (0.86 recall, 92% of tokens removed), a precedent for a 2B model rewriting tool results. DTOC (2609.26121; reversible tool-output compression), U-Fold (2601.18285; intent-aware context folding), *Why Retrying Fails* (Yang, 2605.08563; a failed attempt left in context raises the next attempt's error rate, which bears on discard-and-retry at after_model_call), *Plans Don't Persist* (Mehta et al., 2606.22953; plan signal decays 4.1x in one step), Reclaim Evaluation (2606.25449), AutoCompact (2610.02163), Classifier Context Rot (Martin et al., 2605.12366; 2 to 30x more misses after 800K benign tokens; periodic reminders help). *Measuring Harness-Induced Belief Divergence* (Yi et al., 2607.04528): with task, environment and model fixed, harness choices change the agent's multi-step beliefs.

### 4.10 Decision models, small models and decision-model benchmarks

**Four artifacts are often conflated:**

1. **Jev**: TypeSafe's closed hosted typed-decision model, launched 15 September 2026.
2. **Decision Index 0.2.1** (28 September 2026): a community suite (HF Space multimodalart/jev-decision-index; github.com/apolinario/decision-index), unaffiliated with TypeSafe; Cloudflare's Clef board re-renders it.
3. **JevBench** (v1.5.7): a separate single-maintainer benchmark (benchmarkheaven.com); the Strands team measured its 231 public v1.4.2 tasks.
4. **DecisionBench** (Hanno Labs, 22 September 2026): static accuracy, ECE and NLL.

"Hobson" is the Strands team's internal codename for Strands Decider.

**Decision Index 0.2.1.** 120,340 frozen requests (119,898 scored) from 43 public datasets plus one purpose-built; each request is one typed decision (choice or yes/no) returning per-option probabilities. 38 index benchmarks in five areas: Knowledge and Reasoning 25.85%, Language Understanding 25.85%, Retrieval and Classification 20.0%, Tools and Automation 18.3% (BFCL, ToolRet, API-Bank, Home appliances, When2Call), Arts and Human Taste 10%. Area weights follow sqrt(n); 13 "gold" benchmarks weigh 1.2. Scores are chance-corrected, `(score - chance) / (1 - chance)` clipped to [0, 1]; unanswered requests count as wrong.

- **Calibration is outside the score.** ECE and Brier are published for 32 per-field benchmarks; only ForecastBench uses Brier in the index (about 1.7% of the index).
- **Latency is outside the score.** 750 stratified rows, one request at a time, one RTX PRO 6000, best supported serving path. Jev is timed over HTTPS, so not comparable. The Clef row is self-reported (36 of 38 benchmarks; HLE and iSarcasmEval scored zero): 61.21 against Jev's 57.91.
- **No closed loop.** Every item is a frozen state mapped to a distribution. BFCL and API-Bank are reduced to tool-name selection ("Arguments/order/execution are not evaluated"); When2Call is multiple choice. Six interactive environments (MiniWoB++, Boxoban, RTFM, ScienceWorld, Hanabi, Codenames) are excluded from every index, and would make the decision model the policy rather than a supervisor of an LLM agent.
- **Caveats:** adapted scores are not comparable with native leaderboards; some entrants trained on test items; editions churned 22 to 28 September; the board's "Decider 2B" (card "Mapika Decider-2B", Qwen3.5-2B-Base parent) is not established to be Strands Decider, which is not on the board.

**JevBench v1.5.7.** 1,624 authored decisions (720 sealed); calibration is a scored axis; speed and cost are log-scored; harmonic-mean composite. Single maintainer, short English cases.

**The typed-decision literature (about forty papers, 19 September to 1 October 2026).**

- **Audit and evaluation standards.** *Typed Decision Models: An Early Evidence Audit and Evaluation Checklist* (Tang, 2609.32160): audits 28 papers; a 14-item checklist (label-logprob, cheap-generative-cascade and trained-classifier baselines; option-name invariance; thresholds fit apart from evaluation data; latency with and without caching); finds Jev's clear gains are latency and cost, not accuracy; says per-step accuracy is a weak proxy for end-to-end success and calls for a shared, non-saturated benchmark. This is independent demand for the benchmark proposed here. *Evaluating and Benchmarking the System One Model Jev* (Deußer, 2609.37647): 37 datasets, 346,009 requests. *Benchmarking System One decision models vs trained classifiers for automated decision gates* (Rafe et al., 2610.00346): small trained classifiers win when labels exist; encoder checkpoints do not beat a zero-shot entailment baseline; a 5%-risk threshold still accepts 0.310 of out-of-scope requests; swapping yes/no flips 50.5 answers per 100 for Jev.
- **Security decisions.** *Evaluating System One Models for Agent Security Decisions* (Liu, 2609.33401): Jev, Laya, "Decider" (about 2B, Qwen3.5-2B-Base parent) and Nimble on WAInjectBench, R-Judge and AgentHarm; Decider macro-F1 0.503, 0.368, 0.778 and ECE 0.143, 0.440, 0.173; asymmetric allow / block / escalate thresholds; offline.
- **Calibration of encoder deciders.** *Laya as a Typed Probabilistic Assessor* (Nandakishore, 2609.33843): Laya (ModernBERT-large based) is under-confident (gap -0.214); one temperature (T = 0.469) cuts ECE from 0.204 to 0.037; a frozen escalation gate beats random but misses its 10% error target; preregistered. This matches the under-confidence on long documents in section 3.4.
- **Robustness hazards.** *Type-Safe Is Not Error-Free* (Sun, 2609.26758): renaming options 0/1 to no/yes changes 70.4 answers per 100 and moves AUC from .94 to .23. JevOut (Xu, 2609.30243): natural context additions flip 61.4% of correct decisions. Negation pairs miss summing to 1 by 0.064 (Li, 2609.33209). Probability Contracts (Chen, 2609.37470): event and choice interfaces disagree on 32.8% of pairs. Ordinal-scale bias (Gao, 2609.38827): ordinal scales are compressed. Beyond Answer Confidence (Shankaranarayana, 2610.01006): confidence reaches 0.80 with no relevant information. Decision Hijacking (Wu, 2609.28613): prompt injection raises attack success from 1.8% to 3.5%. Sys1Cal-v1 (Porcedda, 2609.35342): a calibration dataset.
- **Judges and gates.** *JEV-as-a-Judge: Accept When Confident, Escalate When Unsure* (Li, 2609.26550): a frozen cascade scores 92.5 against 93.1 for GPT-6 at 57% of the fee; within 3 points where the verdict is readable from the text, behind where it must be derived; median latency 0.15 s. *JEV vs. LLMs as Rubric Judges* (Rao, 2609.29769): Jev confidence AUROC only .57 to .70, and LLM judges repeat Jev's confident errors 96% of the time. Jev as a judge for agent trace security (Wang, 2609.34862; F1 77.8 against 74.1, median latency 0.99 s hosted). COGNIT-Guard (Chen, 2609.33671): a CPU gatekeeper escalating to Laya-322M under latency and false-positive SLOs (41.6 ms; screens prompts, not actions). LAVOIR (Yilmaz, 2609.30706): a single-pass encoder returning a decision and the value of asking.
- **Uses in loops.** Type-safe decision frameworks for agentic 5G control (Kourtis, 2609.33689): Jev, Laya and AnyJev in a closed-loop testbed with an act / escalate / abstain gate and per-type conformal risk control, with decision type as a variable. REFLEX (section 4.2). Jev in a pentest harness (Barbosa, 2609.28940; 9/13 against 10/13, one run per arm). Jev-Mobile (Zhang, 2609.30186; AndroidWorld 79% against 84%). Harness Tokenomics (Kwartler, 2609.28919). OmniMed-Jev (2610.00381; uses the Choice, Noul and Score vocabulary). Jev on aspect-based sentiment (2609.35293). A weight-free "decision map" with a self-reported JevBench result (Dağlı, 2609.25498; low confidence). LLM2Jev (Li et al., 2610.02076; when and how to fine-tune LLMs into Jev-style decision models) and *Code Owns the Simulation, Jev Owns the Evaluation* (2610.01834; Jev succeeds when the answer is evaluable from what the input states and fails when it requires simulating unstated facts).
- **Small models in the agent harness.** *Measuring the Microtask Eligibility Gap* (Hu, 2610.00025): off-the-shelf Qwen3 0.6B to 8B as harness microtasks (for example auto-approving shell commands): 0 of 16 configurations pass CI-aware thresholds, and quantization does not rescue them. *From Confident Closing to Silent Failure* (Advani, 2606.09863): over 9,876 tau2 trajectories from 8 model families, false success is 45 to 48% of failures in airline and retail and 3% in telecom; LLM judges never exceed AUROC 0.65, while TF-IDF detectors reach 0.83 at 3,300x lower latency. *What Do Compliance Detectors Read?* (Sadhu, 2608.16852): deleting, permuting or substituting the governing rule leaves accuracy unchanged for every guard and probe tested ("rule blindness").
- **Dual-process agents and small models.** SwiftSage (Lin et al., NeurIPS 2023, 2305.17390): a heuristic, not calibrated confidence, chooses between the small and large module. Talker-Reasoner (Christakopoulou et al., 2410.08328): conceptual, no quantitative evaluation. Dualformer (2410.09918). *Small Language Models are the Future of Agentic AI* (Belcak et al., 2506.02153): position paper. GLiGuard (Zaratiana et al., 2605.07982, built on GLiNER2 2507.18546): a 0.3B schema-conditioned encoder in one non-autoregressive pass, competitive F1 with 7 to 27B decoder guards at up to 17x lower latency. Guard models used as gates: Llama Guard (2312.06674), ShieldGemma (2407.21772), Granite Guardian (2412.07724), Qwen3Guard (2510.14276). Function-calling routers: Arch-Router (2506.16655), Hammer (2410.04587), Semantic Router for vLLM (2510.08731). Encoders: ModernBERT (2412.13663); Pointer Networks (1506.03134).
- No arXiv paper was found for the Decision Index, Clef, DecisionBench or Strands Decider.

**What a closed-loop benchmark adds beyond these.** Paired in-loop trials with the gate on and off; scoring the whole system (decider, questions, thresholds, action mapping, agent LLM); on-policy decision sets labeled by counterfactual effect; intervention accounting (helpful against harmful blocks, extra turns, loops after Guide, recovery from a wrong steer); risk-coverage and cost-weighted utility curves; baselines (no gate, prompt or SOP, LLM-judge gate at equal latency, rule gate, oracle gate, random gate at matched block rate); and end-to-end latency and cost per episode.

### 4.13 "Steering" in other senses

In the literature, the bare word "steering" mostly means activation or decoding-time steering (Thinking Intervention 2503.24370; Stolfo et al. 2410.12877; ASA 2602.04935; AxBench 2501.17148; STEER-BENCH 2505.20645), and recently also runtime oversight of agents (LivePlan 2608.06701; Environment Steering 2609.35807). Terms that reach the right audience: runtime enforcement, runtime intervention, inference-time guidance, agent harness, policy-compliant agents, verifier or critic feedback, AI control protocols, agent supervision.

## 5. Novelty assessment

**Covered in the literature:**

- Pre-tool-call enforcement of tau-bench policies by symbolic and LLM verifiers (section 4.2).
- Feedback against blocking, including a clean Deny / generic-Guide / specific-Guide ablation (Environment Steering) and binding against advisory delivery (COTA: forced 16.7 against advice 45.0).
- Threshold operating points, conformal risk control and calibrated deferral (section 4.9).
- Small (0.5B to 2B), fine-tuned or calibrated supervisors in a live loop with closed-loop success: COTA (tau3-Retail), HiSentinel (SWE-bench), REFLEX (Jev on tau2-bench with a threshold sweep), Asymmetric Actor-Critic (4B / 8B / 32B critics on tau-bench).
- Collateral-damage accounting with helpful / neutral / harmful counts: Reinforced Agent, HiSentinel, DAAF, TwinCheck, ContainmentBench.
- Offline score quality not determining closed-loop control: Vasudev, Calibration Is Not Control, What Can Be Enforced?, and replay invalidity (The Replay Gap).
- Trigger / monitor / feedback decomposition with module ablations (HARDE).
- External, state-tracking guidance beating the same content in the prompt (PolicyGuide, JourneyBench, ReIn).

**Thin or absent:**

- **Hook point as an experimental factor.** Multi-hook systems exist (ALTK with six intervention points, CUGA, FinHarness, SafeHarness, PRISM) but none ablates placement. The nearest contrasts are coarse: Life-Harness layers, Vestrum's step against final answer, HARD's context construction against action gate, and the review-window sweep in 2608.23941. IRMA is the only pre-model-call intervention on tau-bench found.
- **Decision type (yes/no, choice, score) as a variable in a live loop.** Typed-decision papers evaluate it offline; COTA varies decision form (pairwise against absolute) but not these types.
- **Threshold sweeps for a supervisor of a fixed agent on a policy benchmark with pass^k.** REFLEX sweeps a threshold on tau2, but as routing to a strong LLM.
- **A length-matched sham message arm in an intervention-policy study.** 2609.20474 uses one for a plan on tau2 retail.
- **The joint factorial** over hook, decision type, wording, message channel, threshold and supervisor size, with paired per-task flips and pass^k, and a cross-hook offline-to-online test on a policy benchmark.
- **Banking (tau-Knowledge) and Haiku-class agents.** FraudBench builds on banking with annotated intervention points but is an attack benchmark; most tau intervention papers use retail or airline with Qwen, GPT or Claude Sonnet actors.

**Verdict.** The mechanism is weakly novel at best; the defensible contribution is characterization. The benchmark fills a real but narrow gap in a crowded and fast-moving area. A reviewer can assemble COTA, Reinforced Agent, Asymmetric Actor-Critic and Calibration Is Not Control as "already done piecemeal", so the contribution has to be the controlled joint design plus the cross-hook offline-to-online test, not any single axis.

## 6. Proposal: a closed-loop benchmark for intervention policies

### 6.1 Positioning

Not a new mechanism and not a decision-model leaderboard: a protocol and harness for evaluating **intervention policies on a fixed agent, environment and task set**, framed as control protocols for reliability. It complements:

- tau-bench, which evaluates agents;
- the Decision Index and SteerBench-Work, which evaluate standalone, single-step judgments;
- AI-control settings, which evaluate adversarial safety.

Name: avoid bare "Steering Bench" (steerbench.com, STEER-BENCH, AxBench). If the name stays, position it explicitly as the end-to-end companion to SteerBench-Work.

### 6.2 Unit of evaluation

An intervention policy `pi = (hook points, decision type and questions, rule, action and message, threshold, supervisor)` applied to a fixed `(agent, environment, tasks, user-simulator seeds)`.

### 6.3 Two tracks

- **Track A, frozen and cheap.** On-policy decision states harvested from recorded trajectories and labeled by counterfactual effect (gold-derived where the environment has gold; prefix branching as in 2606.21399 where it does not). Scores any supervisor, including decision models, without running agents. Seeds from existing assets: 291 recorded banking baseline trials and 684 retail trials. Treat Track A as a screening and prediction track, not as evidence of effect: replayed states rarely stay valid once the agent's actions change (3% in 2608.08239).
- **Track B, live and paired.** Baseline against policy on the same tasks and seeds, at least 4 trials, paired task-level bootstrap clustered by task, Holm correction, and a reported minimum detectable effect. Confirm on fresh or held-out tasks, since extra seeds on the same tasks do not reduce task-sampling error (2608.30916, 2608.11323). Use same-prefix fork-and-continue designs where the environment supports them: exact replay (TwinCheck 2609.26911), prefix branching (2606.21399), replay-until-divergence (AgentCheck 2607.11098), forkable environments (2609.33875), snapshots (DIVERT 2604.21480).
- **Headline measurement:** does the Track A score predict the Track B delta, per hook and per decision type?

### 6.4 Metrics

- Paired change in pass^k and pass@k, with CIs and minimum detectable effect.
- **Primary estimand: intention-to-treat** (policy arm against baseline arm over all trials). Conditioning on whether a trigger fired inside the treated run breaks the pairing (2609.33153); report fired-only or "coordination-active" pass^k (2606.20695) as secondary.
- Per-task flip probabilities estimated from repeated trials (rescued against derailed), rather than run-level flip counts, which mix sampling noise with real harm.
- Helpful / neutral / harmful intervention counts (HiSentinel's 109 / 106 / 27 is the format).
- Intervention precision, recall and timing (Early Intervention Rate, from StepShield).
- Risk-coverage curves over the threshold (AURC, AUGRC), with calibration (ECE, Brier) per decision type.
- Cost: supervisor latency and tokens, agent token and turn inflation; Pareto plots (RouterBench AIQ, RouteLLM CPT and APGR).
- Guide follow-through and retry rates (the banking oracle shows 108 of 234 send-backs changed state).

### 6.5 Anchors and controls

- No steering; always-proceed; a log-only overhead control (already in the repo); an oracle gate (the banking oracle exists); a random gate at matched fire rate; a length-matched sham message (2609.20474); an LLM-judge gate at matched latency; the prompt or SOP equivalent of the same guidance; a tau = 100% arm (decider consulted, never acts).
- **A noise-floor arm:** two configuration-equivalent arms with identical inputs to the agent, to measure the pure run-to-run gap on this agent and task set (2606.20695 found +10 and 0 points on Haiku 4.5 retail, with one +18 that reversed to -3).
- **A blind-resample control:** an unguided rerun from the same state (2608.25920 found unguided reruns repair only 6.90%), separating targeted repair from luck.
- **An against-prior measure** (2608.11727): how often the guidance changed behavior relative to what the agent would have done anyway.
- **Matched-composition controls** for any routing-style arm (2608.14641).
- **Baselines a typed-decision reviewer will expect** (2609.32160, 2610.00346): a label-logprob readout, a cheap generative cascade, a trained classifier, option-name invariance checks (renaming options 0/1 to no/yes changed 70.4 of 100 answers in 2609.26758), thresholds fit on data separate from evaluation, and latency with and without caching.

### 6.6 Domains and agents

- Retail (76% baseline, about 5% of trials addressable) is a poor testbed. Banking has headroom (7.2% baseline, 70.4% oracle). The airline hard set has not been run with a hook.
- Add at least one non-tau environment to avoid the "re-analysis of tau-bench" critique.
- Use more than one agent model. Effects are model-dependent: Vasudev's 26-point drop on one model was about zero on another; paging helps strong models and harms weak ones (2607.11346); COTA's actors adopt only 19 to 33% of advice; Calibration Is Not Control's gains shrink with agent strength (0.293 at Qwen-7B to 0.081 at GPT-5.4).
- Control the user simulator: varying only the simulator moved tau reward by up to 15.2 points (2609.38043) and by up to 9 points on retail (2601.17087), and 24.4% of "successes" contained a user-specification violation.
- Expect headroom to matter: a mid-difficulty task filter cuts task counts by 44 to 70% without changing rankings (2603.23749), and observation-masking gains follow an inverted U against the agent's own accuracy (2606.00408).
- Airline has known policy loopholes on 7 of 50 tasks (2609.14400); a tau2-verified airline subset is used by 2603.29665 and 2607.01426.

### 6.7 Validity hygiene

- Pin the tau version and grading tag: banking_knowledge was re-graded in tau2-bench v1.0.1 (July 2026), and tau3 reportedly edited 29 of 50 airline tasks (2609.14400, citing a blog post).
- Audit Deny and Confirm for vacuous passes (empty responses counted as success, per the Agentic Benchmark Checklist).
- Report errored trials separately; apply the Agentic Benchmark Checklist; add held-out tasks; do log analysis; disclose the harness.
- Use procedure-aware checks alongside reward (2603.29665, 2603.03116).
- Meet the eight claim-specific reporting items of 2609.39989: local decision metrics co-reported with task endpoints are not matched comparisons.
- Account for Deny-then-retry and parallel calls: approval gates let a sibling call execute during a pause in 5 of 6 frameworks (2607.14166); agents circumvent monitors under ordinary task pressure (2609.30217) and infer a hidden monitor from blocking feedback alone (2603.16928); a failed call echoed in context raises the odds of repeating it (2608.23651).
- Fix the grader and simulator issues in section 3.3 before publishing numbers.

### 6.8 Hypotheses the benchmark could settle

These are hypotheses: the repo's data motivates but does not establish them.

- **H1.** Offline decision accuracy, including AUROC, does not predict closed-loop delta. Versions of this are published outside tau and outside multi-hook settings (2602.03338, 2606.21399, 2607.22868); the new content is testing it across hooks and decision types on a policy benchmark with a typed-decision supervisor.
- **H2.** Small supervisors help only on decisions whose answer is visible in the text (surface facts), not on correctness judgments. Supporting evidence: rule blindness in guards and probes (2608.16852), judges reading surface closing language (2606.09863, AUROC at most 0.65), sub-50% pairwise accuracy of small prompted judges on tool steps (2601.12294), Jev failing where the answer must be simulated (2610.01834).
- **H3.** Message content (the exact corrective action) matters more than the control action (Guide against Deny). Supporting evidence: this repo's oracle data (section 3.2), Outcome Monitors, Lucassen, Environment Steering (STOP -36.2, generic retry -10.5, constraint-aware retry +6.5), MCP error messages (naming a callable tool raised recovery from 6% to 88% in one case), COTA (advice 45.0 against forced 16.7).
- **H4.** Hook points other than pre-tool-call have different risk profiles, with the model-call points more prone to collateral damage.
- **H5.** Fire rate times precision on the addressable population predicts net effect better than detector accuracy.
- **H6.** The same guidance lands differently depending on the delivery channel and the agent model: compliance with instructions arriving in tool outputs ranges widely across models (2607.25987, 98.2% to 20.5%), yet error-formatted tool results carry implicit authority (2606.07992). The channel (tool result, injected user message, appended note) should be a measured factor per agent model.
- **H7.** Review granularity matters: verification of one or two actions at a time beats longer review windows, which raise false rejection in step with catch rate (2608.23941). This bears on what the decision model is shown at each hook.

### 6.9 Suggested staging

1. **No new agent runs:** build Track A from recorded trials, score the existing decision modules against it, and check whether the ranking matches the live results already in hand (transfer rule null, wrap-up harmful, oracle large).
2. **Power analysis** for a fractional factorial using `scripts/detectable_lift.py` and the per-task variance from existing baselines.
3. **Live Track B** on banking and the airline hard set, plus one non-tau environment.
4. **Second agent model**, then supervisor-size and threshold sweeps.

## 7. Risks and open questions

- **Timing.** About twenty 2026 papers apply runtime supervision to tau-family tasks, and about forty typed-decision papers appeared in the two weeks from 19 September 2026. The window is closing.
- **Cost and power.** A full factorial is expensive. The repo's detectable-lift simulation puts the detectable effect at about +7 points for banking at 97 tasks x 3 trials with that domain's variance; state that assumption when quoting it. Published estimates for other designs are larger: a paired minimum detectable effect of about 15 points at 100 tasks and one trial on Haiku 4.5 tau2 retail (2606.20695), and tau-bench needing about 25% of its tasks to reproduce a pairwise decision (2607.12338). Many cells make multiplicity bite: on tau2 only 1 of 135 route-level effects survived Holm correction (2608.05778). Tasks, not seeds, are the clustering unit (2608.11323), so budget fresh tasks and cluster the bootstrap by task; adaptive or IRT-based task selection (2609.38914, 2609.21267) is for screening only.
- **Causal identification.** Conditioning on whether a trigger fired inside the treated run breaks the pairing (2609.33153); use intention-to-treat as primary.
- **Generality.** Everything measured so far uses one agent (Haiku 4.5), one simulator and three tau domains. The decision model's size has never been swept independently of the task.
- **Oracle validity.** The oracle is answer-leaked and order-blind in places (24 of 86 failures), so it bounds this hook set, not steering in general.
- **Replay validity.** Recordings ignore guidance, the pass set is small (21 banking passes), and modules were tuned in-sample. Track A labels need held-out tasks.
- **Reviewer framing.** Expect "this is a tau-bench re-analysis". Mitigate with the non-tau environment and by stating the claims the benchmark supports and where it does not (NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations and Datasets guidance).
- **Open question:** does the gain in Outcome Monitors and PolicyGuide come from the hook or from the content of the message? A length-matched sham and a prompt-equivalent arm would separate them.
